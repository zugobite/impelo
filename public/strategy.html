Market Research

A Large Need Is Not Yet A Validated Market.

National evidence explains the access context. Impelo’s small project survey helps frame questions. Neither establishes how many practices will buy this product.

15.5%

Medical-Aid Coverage

Stats SA’s General Household Survey 2025 reports that 15.5% of individuals had medical-aid coverage, approximately 10.0 million people. Coverage differs substantially by province.

Stats SA · GHS 2025 Key Findings, released 26 May 2026. This is a coverage measure, not Impelo’s market size.

85.6%

Household Internet Access

The same survey reports internet access for 85.6% of households. Any household access does not establish a personal smartphone, affordable data, reliable connectivity or digital confidence for every patient.

Stats SA · GHS 2025 Media Release, 26 May 2026.

10 + 7

Exploratory Project Responses

The project surveyed 10 patient/service users and 7 staff in convenience samples. Reported waiting uncertainty and status questions suggest usability and workflow questions to investigate.

Phase 3 Research Context. These are descriptive project findings, not nationally representative statistics or evidence of improved outcomes.

The Design Implication

Keep staff-assisted access, modest page weight and understandable states in the core journey. Research language and device needs at each participating practice. A language dropdown alone does not establish multilingual care. Do not assume a patient without medical aid never uses private care, or that medical-aid membership indicates ability to use a digital portal.

Proposed Entry Market

Start Close To The Workflow.

Validate with a small, reachable practice segment before attempting broad adoption.

First Practices To Explore

Owner-managed outpatient and community-focused practices in Cape Town and Johannesburg, where the team can observe arrival and reception workflows and support a limited pilot. This is a recruitment hypothesis; no clinic relationship is claimed.

Buyer And Daily Users

The likely decision-maker is a practice owner or manager. Reception teams manage arrivals and handoffs; clinicians need permitted context; patients need a next step they can understand. Confirm budgets and purchasing authority in interviews.

Boundaries That Matter

Clinical priority remains a professional decision. Impelo’s proposed value is operational visibility and controlled continuity. Public-sector procurement, complex hospital integrations and national rollout require a separate feasibility assessment.

Positioning Hypothesis

“A clearer visit journey for patients and the teams coordinating their care.” Test whether that proposition is valuable alongside existing practice software. Determine whether practices prefer integration, a narrow workflow tool or a broader replacement before committing to a sales model.

Existing Alternatives

Understand What Practices Already Use.

Compare the workflow and switching cost, not only the feature list. The summaries below describe vendor-published coverage, rather than independently tested performance.

Zanda

Its South African offering describes scheduling, clinical notes, intake, billing, a client portal and telehealth. It is a useful reference for a polished practice experience and practitioner-based subscription structure.

Vendor Plans & Feature Coverage

GoodX

The vendor describes practice administration, billing and claims, scheduling, clinical information and patient-facing services. Discovery should examine how Impelo might fit existing administration and record workflows.

Vendor Product Overview

Healthbridge

The vendor describes billing, practice-management and clinical software, with scheduling and patient-record tools. Explore current system contracts and actual integration needs before proposing migration.

Vendor Product Overview

The Other Alternative: Existing Habits

Ask about paper lists, spreadsheets, phone calls, messaging and in-person status updates without assuming which a practice uses. A familiar process can be harder to replace than a competing product. Document duplicated work, staff training, downtime tolerance and the information that must stay in the clinical system.

Research & Pilot Plan

Turn Assumptions Into Evidence.

This is a proposed research programme. The interviews, task results and pilots below have not yet been conducted.

  1. 01 / Interview The PracticeStart with about 12 interviews across owners, reception staff and clinicians in the two cities. Map arrival, queue updates, record access and exceptions. Recruit for variation in size, connectivity and existing systems; explain the convenience sample and its limits.
  2. 02 / Test With PatientsRun about 12 consented task sessions using synthetic information. Include different language preferences, older users, shared devices and access needs. Test manual search, assisted check-in and status comprehension; do not collect medical histories to evaluate interface usability.
  3. 03 / Establish A BaselineWith practice agreement, record de-identified operational counts: status enquiries, repeated entry, task completion and handoff delays. Define the measurement window, sample and method. Separate clinical priority and appointment mix from software effects.
  4. 04 / Pilot With Clear BoundariesBegin with synthetic-data rehearsal. Move to a limited live pilot only after the legal, security, clinical and support gates below are met. Agree fallback procedures, a rollback owner and how patients receive staff assistance.
  5. 05 / Review The EvidenceCompare baseline and pilot measures with denominators and observation windows. Report task failures and support demand alongside improvements. Small uncontrolled pilots cannot prove a national waiting-time reduction or clinical benefit.
  6. 06 / Decide What Comes NextAsk whether practices will pay, what they need integrated and whether staff can sustain the workflow. Set measurable progression criteria before the pilot. Revise scope if the operational gain does not justify onboarding and support.

Go-To-Market Strategy

A Focused Route To Useful Adoption.

Lead with a credible demonstration, then build through evidence and relationships.

Discover

Use the public website, real-screen demos and targeted, permission-based practice conversations to test the problem and buying process. Offer plain descriptions of current capability, planned work and service limits.

Prove

Recruit a small number of agreed pilot practices with named support owners. Provide staff onboarding and patient-assisted alternatives. Publish a consented case study only when results are measured and approved by the participating practice.

Expand

Extend to similar practices only after reliable operation, manageable support costs and repeatable onboarding are demonstrated. Assess professional associations and integration partnerships; review any referral or endorsement arrangement against applicable professional rules.

Channel And Marketing Rules

Prioritise educational content, consented demonstrations and partner introductions. Direct electronic marketing must follow POPIA section 69, including the relevant consent or qualifying existing-customer conditions and a usable opt-out. Do not target people using health data, present fictional logos as customers, or imply HPCSA endorsement.

Information Regulator · Direct-Marketing Guidance, guidance published 3 December 2024; POPIA, section 69.

Commercial Model To Validate

Price For Sustainable Support.

The prototype demonstrates R499, R1,299 and R2,999 monthly practice tiers, with proposed R0 patient access and a 10% annual reduction. These are invented hypotheses for buyer and cost validation, not approved prices or live contracts.

Value And Packaging

Test practice subscription, per-practitioner and usage-related models with buyers. Compare willingness to pay against the specific workflow value and existing software costs. Assess a patient access route that does not create a new barrier to care.

Cost Floor

Model hosting, storage, backups, messaging, integration maintenance, onboarding, translation, accessibility and support time. Use actual supplier quotes and pilot workload. The academic development budget is not a customer subscription price.

Terms Before Selling

Confirm ZAR pricing, tax wording, included limits, setup fees, data export, cancellation and refunds, service levels and operator responsibilities. Test whether clinics understand the total recurring cost before accepting a paid plan.

Read The Pricing Benchmarks: Welcome Desk’s capacity tiers, Baselayer.med’s solo and practice packages, and Zanda’s practitioner and usage model. Vendor pages reviewed 4 October 2026; coverage differs and prices may change.

No invented total addressable market, revenue forecast or return-on-investment percentage is presented. A bottom-up estimate needs a verified practice count, reachable segment, tested adoption assumptions and an approved price.

Brand Voice

Clear. Human. Precise.

Professional language should reduce uncertainty and preserve dignity, especially when a person is worried, waiting or unfamiliar with digital tools.

Our Voice In Practice

Write short, specific sentences. Name what happened and the next action. Address people respectfully without assumptions about education, age or income. Use South African English in the source copy, with a reviewed glossary for each supported language. Translate complete journeys, including errors and help; 11 spoken languages and SASL need distinct delivery plans.

Use title case for headings and buttons, sentence case for explanations. Keep IBM Plex Sans as the interface voice, Plex Serif for occasional editorial emphasis and Plex Mono for technical identifiers. Lucide icons explain tasks; the pixel-art portraits and stickers bring warmth to welcoming moments. Essential information must remain understandable without artwork or colour.

Waiting

“You’re checked in. Stay nearby; the clinic team will call you.”
State the confirmed stage. Avoid a countdown unless the service has a reliable basis for it.

Interrupted Connection

“We couldn’t confirm this update. Check your connection and try again.”
Do not say the action succeeded until the service confirms it. Preserve an appropriate recovery route.

Access

“This record isn’t available with your current access. Ask your practice administrator for help.”
Explain the boundary without exposing a record, blaming the user or suggesting a bypass.

Sharing

“Review who will receive this information before you continue.”
Name the recipient, purpose and scope in the actual flow. A decorative shield is not a consent explanation.

These are proposed microcopy examples, not legal consent language. Keep reassurance factual. Avoid “guaranteed,” “fully compliant,” “instant care” and unmeasured claims. Reserve playful stickers for welcomes and non-urgent education; keep serious errors and clinical alerts restrained.

South African Launch Readiness

Resolve Responsibility Before Real Patient Data.

These are implementation gates for a future live service. The demo’s Legal & Privacy pages describe its present behaviour, without declaring production compliance.

  1. Operator, Notice And AccountabilityIdentify the genuine operator, responsible parties and Information Officer. Publish actual contact and supplier details. Complete the applicable PAIA manual and a POPIA section 18 notice matching real collection purposes, recipients, rights and retention.
  2. Lawful Health And Child ProcessingDocument the ordinary processing grounds and the applicable special-information authorisation. Determine the software operator’s role rather than assuming a clinician’s exception applies to it. Confirm caregiver authority and the lawful conditions for children’s information.
  3. Confidentiality And AccessReview National Health Act confidentiality and record duties with clinic partners. Enforce least privilege and practice scope on the service, with accountable audit access. Cross-practice sharing needs an assessed lawful basis and clear patient-facing choices.
  4. Safeguards And VendorsAssess risks, recovery, secure transport/storage, authentication and shared-device sessions. Sign written operator agreements where required. Inventory subprocessors and transfers; assess POPIA section 72 conditions without assuming a blanket local-hosting requirement.
  5. Retention And RightsAgree a record-category schedule against current legal and professional requirements, with lawful holds and secure disposal. Provide genuine access, correction, objection and applicable deletion routes. Avoid promising automatic deletion of every clinical record.
  6. Incidents And Breach ResponseSet an escalation owner and response procedure. POPIA section 22 uses “as soon as reasonably possible,” not a universal 72-hour rule. Responsible-party notices to the regulator and affected people follow the statutory conditions; operators promptly notify the responsible party. The regulator directs reports through its eServices portal from 1 April 2025.
  7. Consumer Terms And MarketingPublish genuine ECTA supplier disclosures before applicable online sales. Use clear, fair terms and substantiated claims under the CPA. Set cancellation/refund terms for the actual service and applicable law. Review direct marketing against POPIA section 69.
  8. Clinical And Operational Sign-OffConfirm the clinical boundaries, priority owner and safe downtime process. Have appropriately qualified South African reviewers assess the actual contracts, data flows and professional requirements. Test controls and staff readiness before a live pilot; policy wording alone does not implement compliance.

Primary references: POPIA (sections 8–25, 26–35, 69 and 72); National Health Act (records and confidentiality, sections 13–17); Information Regulator PAIA; POPIA breach guidance and eServices direction; ECTA; CPA; HPCSA recordkeeping guidance. Professional requirements apply to the relevant registered professionals; no HPCSA approval of Impelo is claimed.
