CURRENT FOUNDATION

An honest view of the starting point.

The current portal provides the foundation. This guide describes the intended service beyond that foundation.

Existing interface foundation

Authentication screens, profile, role-based navigation, dashboard shortcuts, the users-list demo, new icon, green theme, pixel scenery, and atlas avatars are represented in the local project.

Planned care workflows

Queue, appointments, records, practices, patient lookup, and reports are currently marked coming soon in the navigation model. Their end-to-end clinical behaviour is not established by these previews.

Public Prototype In Place

The five public pages, six named team profiles, project research, language previews and local enquiry demo are implemented. A live contact service, approved commercial terms, reviewed translations and a production accessibility statement remain delivery work.

DELIVERY PHASES

Build complete journeys in manageable phases.

Sequence with clinic partners and the people using the service. These are proposed phases, not promised dates.

  1. 1 / Discovery & responsibility

    Confirm participating practices, service boundaries, clinical decision owners, product operator, support responsibilities, and what success means. Map current clinic procedures and inclusion barriers.

  2. 2 / Public website & trust

    Publish the verified platform explanation, builder information, approved pricing or transparent holding copy, functioning contact route, and reviewed policy content. Use the new icon, palette, and art.

  3. 3 / Account & access foundation

    Validate identity, roles, recovery, profile, language preferences, permissions, and shared-device sessions. Test essential account journeys with assistive technology.

  4. 4 / Clinic arrival & queue pilot

    Complete reception-assisted and patient check-in, duplicate prevention, staff queue actions, patient states, timestamped updates, and poor-connection recovery in a small agreed pilot.

  5. 5 / Appointments & continuity

    Add availability, confirmation, change/cancellation, published records, and reviewed sharing flows. Confirm conflicts, audit needs, support paths, and integration responsibilities.

  6. 6 / Broader inclusion & operational scale

    Expand reviewed language coverage, SASL resources, practice administration, and operational reports. Scale only after pilot evidence, access checks, performance results, and support readiness.

SCREEN QUALITY

A common contract for every screen.

A feature is ready when its real journey works, including the moments that do not go to plan.

Entry & permission

Define who can reach the screen, practice context, required identity, and permission checks. Enforce access on the service as well as the interface.

Content & action

Specify the information source, freshness, fields, one primary action, and how a person knows it succeeded. Use plain language and labelled icons.

States & recovery

Specify initial, loading, empty, success, invalid input, service error, connection loss, session expiry, and access denied. Do not reuse one generic message for all failures.

Accessibility & language

Check keyboard, focus, screen reader labels, contrast, reflow, error feedback, translated content, and reduced motion for the complete task.

Data & device cost

Measure initial page weight, task latency, media cost, and behaviour on slow connections and supported lower-end phones. A low-data option should prevent optional downloads.

Privacy & support

Review visible identifiers, shared-device behaviour, logs, retained drafts, and downloads. Define who helps when a user cannot complete the task.

ACCEPTANCE EXAMPLES

Make readiness observable.

Use outcome-based checks that can be repeated with representative users.

Booking

A patient can review a visit, submit it once, and distinguish a confirmed booking from an interrupted request. A retry cannot create an accidental duplicate.

Queue

A patient can identify their current stage and next action. When updates stop, the last confirmed time and stale state are visible. A clinic priority change is understandable.

Permissions

A person can identify the recipient and scope of optional sharing, decline it, and find its history. Staff without permission cannot access the protected record.

Language

A supported-language user can complete the whole priority journey, including errors and help, without encountering an unlabelled English fallback.

Accessibility

A person can complete the priority journey using keyboard and screen reader, at the target narrow width and zoom, with meaningful focus and status feedback.

Contact

The visitor receives a receipt only after a message is accepted. Failure preserves the appropriate draft and shows an actual alternative contact route.

OPEN DECISIONS

Decisions to resolve before launch.

Keep this register alongside the design direction. Confirm business and service facts before replacing the planned labels.

  • Ownership: the six student builders are named; the future operator, partners, clinical responsibilities and support owners still need confirmation.
  • Public content: the team names and project roles are confirmed; biographies, contact channels, hours and response commitments remain to be supplied.
  • Commercial model: patient costs, practice tiers, ZAR amounts, tax wording, onboarding, and cancellation terms.
  • Care operations: walk-ins, scheduling rules, check-in validation, queue priority, missed calls, and assisted alternatives.
  • Identity & delegation: enrolment requirements, no-email alternatives, account recovery, caregivers, and role assignments.
  • Language coverage: release priorities, reviewers, glossary, fallback, SASL resources, and support capacity.
  • Data responsibilities: permission model, reviewed privacy and retention policies, record publication, audit, and integrations.
  • Access & devices: supported browsers, performance budgets, low-data behaviour, accessibility audit scope, and barrier handling.

DESIGN HANDOFF

Carry the same visual direction into delivery.

Warmth should help the task feel approachable without competing with clinical information.

Identity

Use the icon in auth and sidebar while preserving existing structure. Use full logo lockups in documents and the identity reference.

Colour & meaning

Forest anchors text and navigation; green leads primary actions; sage supports surfaces. Verify contrast. Status needs a word and, where useful, an icon as well as colour.

Art & avatars

Use dimensional pixel art with South African context at welcoming moments. Keep full avatar heads visible. Art must be optional, responsive, and removable for low-data use.

Icons & microcopy

Use the shared Lucide direction consistently. Label essential actions; make help friendly and specific. Keep playful accents away from urgent clinical or security warnings.
