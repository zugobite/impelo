VISITOR PATHS

One Public Website. Three Visitor Journeys.

Visitors need to understand what Impelo does before deciding whether it is relevant to them.

A person seeking care

Understand the experience → learn how access works → find participating practice information → sign in when available. Do not require an account to read basic information.

A clinic considering Impelo

Understand clinic workflows → assess suitability → read confirmed pricing and inclusions → contact the team about onboarding.

A partner or collaborator

Understand the problem and product stage → meet the people behind it → see the inclusion commitments → send a relevant enquiry.

HOMEPAGE SECTIONS

What each homepage section must answer.

Use the brand to create recognition, then make the product understandable in plain language.

  1. Hero: what is it?

    One clear sentence explaining the healthcare queue and patient information platform. Use the new icon and green scenery, with one patient route and one clinic route. Show the actual launch stage.

  2. Problem: why does it exist?

    Explain uncertainty during visits, fragmented information, and busy clinic handoffs. Avoid statistics or outcome claims without evidence.

  3. Platform: what can it do?

    Introduce queue visibility, appointments, records, and practice context with labelled screenshots. Distinguish available capabilities from roadmap concepts.

  4. How it works: what happens next?

    Show a short patient sequence and a clinic sequence. Link to fuller explanations; make assisted check-in and offline alternatives visible once confirmed.

  5. People: who benefits?

    Describe patients, reception teams, practitioners, and administrators in terms of practical tasks. Explain permission boundaries.

  6. Inclusion: will it work for me?

    State actual language coverage, accessibility features, supported devices, data requirements, and contact alternatives. Link to a maintained accessibility statement.

  7. Team: who is responsible?

    Introduce the builders and ownership with verified names, roles, and biographies. Separate advisors, contributors, and clinical partners accurately.

  8. Pricing: what will it cost?

    Show approved practice costs, any patient charges, inclusions, exclusions, billing periods, and onboarding terms. Before approval, use a transparent enquiry route.

  9. Contact: how do I speak to someone?

    Offer clear topics and confirmed reply channels. Explain who handles clinical concerns versus product questions.

  10. Footer: where are the essentials?

    Repeat navigation, sign-in, privacy, terms, accessibility, language access, and confirmed business contact information.

PRICING

Transparent pricing, with access in mind.

The prototype now demonstrates Patient Access at R0, Practice Essentials at R499/month, Practice Team at R1,299/month and Clinic Network at R2,999/month. Annual examples apply a proposed 10% reduction. These invented amounts and future inclusions are a discussion framework, not available subscriptions.

Patient access

The example model keeps patient visit details, queue stages, published information and sharing choices at R0. Validate sustainable funding and assisted access; healthcare services and mobile data remain separate costs.

Practice subscription

Essentials proposes one location and 5 staff accounts. Team proposes one location and 15 staff accounts, with multi-practitioner scheduling, team handoffs and filtered reporting. Role permissions, consent and audit history are foundations across paid plans.

Multi-site & onboarding

Network proposes up to 3 locations and 40 staff accounts across the organisation, with site-specific queues, consent-managed record requests, combined reporting and phased rollout support. Messaging, complex migration, custom integrations and hardware are separately scoped.

Before any price goes live

  • Approve amounts in South African rand and the billing period.
  • Specify whether VAT is included, where applicable, using reviewed billing wording.
  • Define trial or pilot terms, cancellation, renewal, and support scope.
  • Disclose optional onboarding, message, migration, or integration charges.
  • Explain what happens to access and data when a subscription ends.
  • Evaluate affordability with small practices and communities facing limited resources.

Explore The Example Plans And Research. Amounts assume VAT inclusion for comparison; tax treatment, service availability, support hours and commercial terms require validation before launch.

CONTACT

A contact experience that routes people correctly.

The contact section is for product and practice enquiries. Its production service and contact details still need to be supplied.

Form structure

Name
A visible label and a clear required marker.
Reply email
Explain the format. Decide whether a supported phone alternative is available.
Topic
Practice enquiry · partnership · account help · accessibility feedback · other.
Organisation
Optional, relevant to a practice or partnership enquiry.
Message
Ask for the enquiry, with a clear length limit. Tell visitors not to send medical records or urgent health details.

Submission & support states

Provide a reviewable privacy notice, then an unambiguous Send enquiry action. Show a receipt only after the service accepts the message.

If sending fails, keep the non-sensitive draft, explain the problem, and offer a confirmed alternative channel. Avoid forcing a visitor to solve a visual puzzle.

Only display operating hours and response-time commitments the team can actually meet. Define who owns each topic and how accessibility issues are escalated.

Clinical concerns: direct patients to their clinic’s confirmed care channels. This marketing form must not be presented as a clinical or emergency service.
